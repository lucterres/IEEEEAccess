# Blind Discrimination Experiment Protocol

## Objective

Evaluate whether expert geoscientists can distinguish between **real** and **synthetic** seismic images and whether the synthetic images generated by the proposed method preserve sufficient geological realism to support expert segmentation.

## Relation to the manuscript

This protocol follows the procedure described in the revised manuscript subsection **"Blind Discrimination Experiment Protocol"** in `_v7.tex`.

## Experimental design

In the redesigned evaluation, real and synthetic seismic images from the TGS Salt Identification Challenge dataset are presented to expert geoscientists in a **single, randomized, interleaved sequence**.

Experts are **not informed** of the origin of each image prior to evaluation.

For each image, the experts are asked to:
1. classify the image as **real** or **synthetic**;
2. identify and segment the **salt body regions** using a graphics computer program.

This design constitutes a formal blind discrimination test, providing evidence directly relevant to the hypothesis that the synthetic images are virtually indistinguishable from real seismic images by geoscience domain experts.

## Participants

- Number of evaluators: **3 geoscientists**
- Profile: professionals who routinely work in seismic analysis and interpretation

## Control images

Control images **without any saline body** are included in the stimulus set to assess the experts' baseline response tendency.

## Randomization

The identity and sequence of images must be **fully randomized across participants** to avoid order effects.

For each evaluator:
1. generate a randomized presentation order;
2. interleave real and synthetic images in a single sequence;
3. include control images in the same randomized sequence;
4. avoid any filename, folder name, or metadata that may reveal image origin.

## Image set preparation

Prepare the experiment image pool using the TGS Salt Identification Challenge dataset and the synthetic images produced by the proposed method.

For each image, register in `blind_discrimination_image_manifest.csv`:
- `image_id`
- `image_filename`
- `image_source_ground_truth`
- `true_has_salt`
- `ground_truth_mask_file`
- `randomization_pool`

## Session procedure

### Step 1 — Briefing

Before the session:
- inform the evaluator that they will inspect seismic images one at a time;
- explain that, for each image, they must classify it as `real` or `synthetic` and segment the salt body region;
- do **not** reveal image origin before evaluation.

### Step 2 — Image-by-image evaluation

For each image, the evaluator should record:
- `predicted_source` = `real` or `synthetic`
- `segmentation_mask_file`
- `segmentation_done` = `yes` or `no`
- `notes`

### Step 3 — Segmentation task

For each image, the evaluator identifies and marks the salt body region using the selected graphics computer program.

Store the resulting segmentation mask and register its path in:
- `segmentation_mask_file`

## Recorded outputs

The main experiment output is:
- the completed `blind_discrimination_experiment_template.csv`
- the segmentation mask files produced by the evaluators

Each row in the CSV corresponds to one:
- **image × evaluator**

## Post-processing

The segmentation masks produced by the experts must be compared against the ground-truth masks using the measures described in `_v7.tex`:
- precision
- recall
- F1-score

These metrics should be computed after the experiment and recorded in the CSV.

## Primary analyses

### A. Blind discrimination performance

Measure the experts' ability to distinguish real from synthetic images:
- classification accuracy for `predicted_source`
- confusion matrix for real vs. synthetic
- per-evaluator and aggregated results

### B. Segmentation performance

Compare the segmentation masks produced by the experts against the ground-truth masks using:
- precision
- recall
- F1-score

Report results separately for:
- real images
- synthetic images

## Bias control

To remain consistent with the manuscript protocol:
- use a single randomized interleaved sequence;
- keep evaluators blinded to image origin;
- randomize image identity and order across participants;
- include control images without salt bodies.
