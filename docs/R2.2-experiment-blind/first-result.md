To evaluate the results, the measures described in Section `eval:meas:sect` were calculated for each expert and for the group of experts, and their averages and standard deviations were consolidated. Table `real_images_evaluation` presents these evaluations for 20 real seismic images, reporting an average precision score of **0.88761**. This indicates that the experts identified saline rock portions with **88.7%** accuracy, while **11.3%** of the regions labeled as salt actually corresponded to common rock. The average recall of **0.87795** shows that **87.7%** of the saline rock portions were effectively identified, leaving **12.3%** of the salt portions undetected. Based on these values, the specialists achieved an average **F1-score of 0.88159** for the real seismic images.

Table `synthetic_images_evaluation` shows the evaluation results for 30 synthetic seismic images. The average precision was **0.87539**, indicating that the experts correctly identified **87.5%** of the synthetic saline rock portions, while **12.5%** of the regions marked as salt were actually common rock. The average recall was **0.86536**, meaning that **86.5%** of the synthetic saline rock portions were successfully identified, whereas **13.5%** were missed. The resulting average **F1-score was 0.86901** for the synthetic images, considering all expert evaluations.

Comparing the geoscience specialists’ evaluations of real and synthetic seismic images, the difference is less than **2%** in favor of the real images. From the specialists’ perspective, the synthetic seismic images are therefore virtually indistinguishable from the real ones. In this sense, the synthetic images can be considered comparable to real seismic images.
