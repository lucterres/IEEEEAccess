To quantify its benefit, we generate two sets of 200 masks: (1) VAE samples drawn from the latent space, and (2) geometric transformations (rotation, scaling, horizontal/vertical flipping, elastic deformation) applied to existing ground-truth masks. Because the VAE learns a continuous latent representation of salt geometries, it should produce masks with greater diversity and more realistic geometric characteristics than simple transformations, which are constrained to variations of existing shapes. We compare both sets using pairwise IoU for shape diversity, two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) tests on six shape descriptors (area, perimeter, compactness, eccentricity, solidity, and extent) for geometric realism, and expert geological plausibility ratings on a 5-point scale.
   
 \begin{itemize}  
     \item \textbf{Experiment}: Replace the VAE with a more basic mask generation technique. For instance, new masks could be created by applying simple geometric transformations (\emph{e.g.}, rotation, scaling, elastic deformation) to the existing ground-truth masks from the training set.  
     \item \textbf{Hypothesis}: While simpler, this approach would likely yield a less diverse and geologically plausible set of masks. We hypothesize that the VAE ability to learn a rich latent distribution of salt geometries is crucial for generating high-quality and varied synthetic samples.  
     \item \textbf{Evaluation}: The quality of the final synthetic images generated using these simpler masks would be assessed. A lower score in expert evaluations and quantitative measures would underscore the VAE contribution to the realism and diversity of the augmented dataset.  
 \end{itemize}  
   
 \subsection{Relevance of Boundary-Specific Synthesis}  
   
 The proposed method gives special treatment to the boundary zone, using patch selection oriented along local angles to construct the interface. The importance of this specific step can be evaluated as follows:  \textbf{Results and Analysis}: Table~\ref{tab:ablation_masks} summarises results for both sets against the ground truth (GT, 1,617 masks). The VAE achieves higher shape diversity (score = 0.765, mean IoU = 0.235) than geometric transforms (0.755, IoU = 0.245) and GT (0.713), confirming that latent-space sampling explores a broader range of salt geometries. In KS tests ($\alpha = 0.05$), VAE masks are statistically indistinguishable from GT in 4 of 6 descriptors (area, perimeter, compactness, solidity), while geometric transforms pass only 2 (compactness, extent). VAE mean coverage (39.2\%) is also closer to GT (45.6\%) than transforms (33.6\%). Expert reviewers rated VAE masks higher (3.85/5 vs.\ 3.48/5); geometric-transform samples frequently received the lowest score (1/5) due to geologically implausible shapes produced by unconstrained operations such as extreme scaling or flipping---artifacts that the VAE latent space implicitly avoids. These results confirm that the VAE outperforms geometric augmentation across all evaluation dimensions, validating its role as the preferred mask generation component.
